# ⚔️ Chapter 05: The Phantom Alliance

## The Weight of War

Weeks had passed since General Phir's cavalry rode out of the city gates. The fires of preparation burned day and night.

The Archery Ground rang with the twang of bowstrings from dawn till dusk. The Explosive Arena echoed with controlled blasts as demolitionists perfected their craft. The Siege Workshop never slept — catapults rose from timber and iron, one after another. The Barracks, once empty, now housed every able-bodied citizen who had answered the call.

But it was not enough.

Every scout that returned brought the same news: the POP army was vast. Thousands upon thousands of trained soldiers. War machines. Cavalry. Veterans who had fought a hundred battles.

The OOP city had near a hundred fighters. Brave, determined — but near a hundred.

The catapults were good. The bows were sharp. The explosives were ready. But everyone knew — numbers mattered. And the numbers said they would lose.

A single question echoed through every home, every street, every whispered conversation at the Tavern:

*Can we win?*

## The Strategy

In a small room above the Siege Workshop, the young people who had first stepped forward that night gathered around a table. Maps were spread across the surface — the valley, the eastern plains, the ridge lines. They marked positions, drew arrows, calculated distances.

The young man who had spoken first — the one who had challenged General Phir — stood at the head. His name was **Kael**. He had been a carpenter's apprentice before all of this. Now he was the closest thing the city had to a commander.

"We can't match them head-on," he said, tracing the eastern approach. "They'll expect us to defend the city. But if we meet them on the plains, we lose our advantage. The valley narrows near the eastern ridge. We can funnel them."

"Funnel them into what?" asked Mira, the woman with the bow who had stepped forward beside him.

Kael hesitated. He didn't have an answer.

A voice came from the doorway — soft, but carrying a weight that silenced the room.

"Funnel them into a trap."

Everyone turned.

## The Visitor

Standing in the doorway was a woman none of them had seen before. She was tall, draped in a travel-worn cloak of olive green. Dust covered her boots. A scarf hid the lower half of her face. But her eyes — sharp, observant, ancient — swept across the room and took in everything in a single glance.

Behind her, two guards in unfamiliar armor stood watch. Their shields bore a crest no one in the room recognized — an elephant, silver on olive green.

"I need to speak with the people who lead this war," she said. "In private."

Kael exchanged glances with Mira. Then he nodded.

The others filed out. The door closed.

What was said in that room, no one else heard. But when the meeting ended, Kael emerged with something no one had seen on his face since the night of the warning:

Hope.

## The Day of War

Dawn broke grey and cold on the eastern plains.

The OOP army stood in formation — barely a hundred strong. Archers from the Archery Ground stood at the flanks. Demolitionists clutched their explosives, ready. The catapults had been rolled into position, their wooden frames groaning under tension.

Across the field, the POP army stretched as far as the eye could see. Thousands of soldiers. Rows of steel that glinted in the pale morning light. Banners of grey and crimson snapped in the wind. War machines — larger than anything OOP had built — lined their rear.

General Phir sat atop his horse at the center of the formation, watching the tiny OOP army with cold amusement.

The silence stretched. Then a lone rider from the POP lines trotted forward, bearing a white flag.

---

The peace talks lasted only minutes.

The terms were simple: surrender, disband the army, and hand over the velfire mines. In exchange, the city would be spared.

Kael refused.

The rider returned to the POP lines. Drums began to beat. The ground trembled as thousands of boots began to march.

The OOP soldiers gripped their weapons. Some prayed. Some closed their eyes. None stepped back.

Kael raised his sword.

*"Stand firm. This is our home."*

## The Phantom

The POP army was a hundred paces away. Fifty. Twenty.

And then the ground *vibrated*.

It started as a low rumble — barely noticeable over the thunder of marching feet. But it grew. Steady. Deep. The kind of vibration that came from something massive moving in unison.

The POP soldiers slowed. Confusion rippled through their ranks.

Kael lowered his sword. A smile crossed his face.

From the far west of the battlefield — the direction no one had been watching — a cloud of dust rose against the horizon. It grew larger, closer, until shapes emerged from within.

Horses. Hundreds of them. Cavalry in polished armor, their shields bearing the silver elephant on olive green. Behind them, ranks of infantry stretching wide — spearmen, swordsmen, archers. And at the center, a banner none of the OOP people had ever seen, but which they would never forget.

Olive green. Silver elephant. The colors of the **Phantom Kingdom of the West**.

The rider who had visited Kael in secret now rode at the front of that army. Her scarf was gone. She wore a commander's armor, and her voice carried across the battlefield like a war horn:

*"The Phantom Kingdom honors its alliances. OOP, you do not fight alone."*

General Phir's face — for the first time — lost its cold amusement.

The OOP soldiers erupted. What had been a desperate stand was now a battle with hope.

---

*The Phantom Kingdom had agreed in secret to join the war against the tyranny of POP. The alliance had been forged in whispers, in a small room above a workshop, between a carpenter's apprentice and a commander from a distant land. Now their armies stood together. The war for the valley was no longer one-sided. And the forces of POP, for the first time in centuries, faced an enemy that would not be easily crushed.*
